# How to Spot AI-Generated Images in Five Practical Steps

You can identify AI-generated images by combining metadata analysis, reverse image searches, and specialized detection tools — though none are completely reliable on their own. Modern AI detectors often produce inconsistent results when analyzing cutting-edge generated images, so combining multiple techniques is essential. The encouraging part: several dependable indicators exist, and I'll show you how to apply them effectively.

## Start with the foundation: Embedded credentials and origin markers

**Check for embedded credentials and origin information before using any detection software. Google's SynthID and OpenAI's C2PA represent your strongest signals — a verified credential confirms the source. Without either, the image likely came from freely available models or earlier generation tools, requiring alternative verification methods.**

This is your best starting point because embedded markers are the most trustworthy indicator. Open the image in your browser and navigate to [contentcredentials.org](https://contentcredentials.org) — upload your file to look for C2PA Content Credentials. A verified credential from OpenAI, Adobe, or Google offers compelling proof of AI creation (or legitimate AI-assisted editing). Missing credentials don't confirm the image is authentic; they simply mean the watermark was removed or never added initially.

For images from Google, check [Google's SynthID detector](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/synthid-identifying-images-created-by-generative-ai/) — it identifies concealed digital watermarks that Google integrates into Gemini and Imagen creations.

## Tracing the origin: Reverse image searching

**Reverse image searching won't identify AI generation itself, but it reveals a widespread problem: authentic photographs that have been partially rewritten with AI or combined with other images. Searching across multiple platforms helps uncover the original source or earlier iterations.**

Try [Google Lens](https://lens.google.com/), TinEye, or [Bing Visual Search](https://www.bing.com/visualsearch). Paste or upload the image. When you locate previous versions missing certain features, or when you discover a "source" tied to a suspicious origin (a stock image attributed to an unverifiable creator), that warrants closer examination.

## Step-by-step: Using automated detection systems

1. **Access an online detection platform** — Test Sensity, [AI or Not](https://www.aiornotai.com/), or [Optic](https://opticca.com/). All are browser-based and available at no cost.

2. **Submit your image** — Use click-and-drag or browse your device. When available, use the original uncompressed version; compressed files can reduce detector accuracy.

3. **Interpret the confidence percentage carefully** — Most platforms display a score (e.g., "87% confidence this is AI-generated"). Reliability fluctuates based on compression level and the image's source, so view scores as one piece of evidence rather than a final answer.

4. **Study the explanation provided** — Some tools like Optic highlight specific areas flagged by the detector (blurry hands, strange lighting, unnatural surfaces). Look for recurring issues: AI creations frequently display odd hair textures, unusual teeth, or strange background elements.

5. **Cross-check with multiple detectors** — Agreement between two separate tools strengthens your conclusion. When tools produce different results, the image remains inconclusive; relying on a single detector introduces too much uncertainty.

6. **Inspect file information manually** — Right-click the image and choose Properties (Windows) or Get Info (macOS). Authentic photographs typically include EXIF information (camera specifications, ISO, shutter speed, location data). AI images usually lack this data. Use [ExifTool](https://exiftool.org/) or web-based EXIF tools for comprehensive analysis.

7. **Study zoomed-in details** — Magnify to 200% and examine hands, teeth, hair, written content, and surroundings. Generated images sometimes reveal impossible physical features, incomplete or merged fingers, gibberish writing, or repetitive unnatural patterns, though newer systems are becoming increasingly sophisticated.

8. **Recognize this as an ongoing competition** — AI systems improve continuously, and detection methods must adapt accordingly. No tool maintains consistent effectiveness over extended periods.

## Understanding detection limitations: Vendor promises versus real-world performance

**Companies promote impressive accuracy claims, yet actual-world tests reveal that current detection faces major obstacles with newer AI models. The difference between controlled testing results and practical field results is dramatic, particularly when images experience recompression from social platforms. The constant evolution between creation and detection means solutions need regular refinement.**

Platform-based recompression significantly impairs detection accuracy when images are shared on social networks. A detector calibrated for an unmodified file may perform inadequately after recompression occurs.

**The essential point:** A single detector cannot identify all AI images reliably. Combine multiple approaches — verify watermarks initially, perform reverse searches to find originals, employ 2–3 automated systems, and manually review file data and pixel-level characteristics. When results prove ambiguous, acknowledge that ambiguity instead of trusting a tool's certainty rating. Detection methods continue advancing; keep yourself updated on emerging capabilities and constraints.
