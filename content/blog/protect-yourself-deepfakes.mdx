# Protecting Yourself from Deepfakes: A Five-Step Defense Plan

You can shield yourself from deepfakes using five actionable strategies: actively track your online presence, apply identifying marks to your original content, create detailed records of incidents, file complaints with social media companies, and familiarize yourself with applicable laws. This guide covers each tactic so you can take preventive action before a deepfake emerges—or respond decisively if one already exists.

## How to protect yourself from deepfakes: a five-step defense plan

**1. Set up continuous image monitoring**

Google Alerts and reverse image search serve as essential early warning systems. Establish a Google Alert using your full name and any common spelling variations. Create additional alerts combining your name with terms like "deepfake" or "intimate image." For visual content, use Google Images' reverse search feature monthly—upload a clear photo of yourself and review the results for any unauthorized reproductions. If you're dealing with sexually explicit imagery created without consent, specialized monitoring platforms such as Thorn and the Cyber Civil Rights Initiative can help by scanning various online spaces for abusive material featuring your likeness.

**2. Watermark and tag original media**

Embed identifying information (EXIF data) into your photos and approved images. Tools like ExifTool let you insert copyright notices and timestamps—creating a verifiable record of when and by whom an image was created. For significant images (professional photos, formal portraits), apply a visible watermark showing your name and date. While this technique won't prevent deepfake creation, it establishes a clear connection between you and legitimate versions of your image, strengthening your position when contesting fraudulent versions.

**3. Document everything immediately**

When you encounter a deepfake or non-consensual image of yourself, take screenshots showing the URL, exact time, and the specific platform. Preserve the original file along with all associated data. Record the location where you found it, the discovery time, and any information about how widely it had spread. Keep backup copies in secure storage (such as encrypted cloud services or password-protected directories). This documentation becomes invaluable for submitting formal complaints to platforms and supporting law enforcement investigations—both require clear evidence trails.

**4. Report to platforms within 48 hours**

Federal legislation requires social media companies to remove non-consensual intimate imagery—whether authentic photographs or AI-generated fakes—once they receive a report from the affected person. Submit complaints using your platform's official reporting system (all major platforms including [Facebook](/ai-facebook-post-generator), [Instagram](/ai-instagram-post-generator), [TikTok](/ai-tiktok-caption-generator), and [X](/ai-twitter-post-generator) have dedicated channels). In your report, specify that the content is non-consensual and sexually explicit, include your documented evidence, and clarify whether the image is a deepfake. Document when you submitted the report and save any confirmation numbers provided.

**5. Know your legal remedies by region**

In the **US**, federal law enables you to request takedown of non-consensual intimate imagery. California's laws specifically criminalize the creation and distribution of sexually explicit deepfakes without consent. Additional proposed federal legislation would create a civil lawsuit option allowing victims to seek substantial damages for violations. In the **European Union**, digital platform regulations mandate the removal of prohibited content, including non-consensual intimate images. The **UK** enforces online safety standards requiring similar content removal. Various countries are introducing their own protections—including provisions allowing victims to seek financial compensation for unauthorized deepfakes, sometimes extending rights protection for decades. Laws addressing deepfakes are evolving rapidly across jurisdictions, so checking your local regulations is essential.

## The emotional reality behind the statistics

Non-consensual deepfakes inflict serious and enduring emotional damage that numbers alone cannot convey. Victims experience profound feelings of violation, psychological trauma, fractured relationships, and damaged reputations that multiply with each new share. Young people face particular vulnerability to these harms, often experiencing extortion and social rejection alongside the initial violation. The cumulative effect on mental health and wellbeing can be devastating.

If you're experiencing this situation, know that you're not the first and the responsibility lies entirely with those who created and shared the content—never with you. Organizations like the Cyber Civil Rights Initiative provide confidential support and practical assistance at no cost. Taking the step to report is emotionally challenging but crucial; each report strengthens the evidence base that platforms and authorities use to respond effectively.
