PART 2: LEAFLET PUBLISHING GUIDE
========================================

How Leaflet works for Klinchapp

Leaflet is added as a publishing destination in Klinchapp's existing automated pipeline — the same infrastructure that publishes to WordPress and Blogger. When Kira generates a post and it publishes to klinchapp.com, the same pipeline triggers a Leaflet publish via the Standard.site AT Protocol API. No manual step. No separate workflow.

What happens automatically once published on Leaflet:
- Appears in the Bluesky timeline for followers and discoverable by the Atmosphere community
- Appears in Leaflet's Reader and Standard Reader
- Openly crawlable by AI engines across the open social web

Structure of a Leaflet post (auto-generated by Kira alongside each blog post):
- Answer-first paragraph (already being added to main blog — same content)
- 3 to 4 key sections from the post
- FAQ block (already being added to main blog — same content)
- Link to the full post on klinchapp.com for depth

Thread 2 — How to Train AI on Your Writing Style
----------------------------------------
Format: Practical guide | Best posted: Wednesday

Post 1 (hook):
Generic AI writing sounds like generic AI writing.

Here's the fix: a system prompt that takes 2-3 hours to build and makes every AI output sound like you from that point forward.

Five steps 🧵

Post 2:
Step 1: Collect 5-10 pieces of writing you've actually published.

Pick work where you felt most in voice. Different formats help — a blog post AND an email AND a social caption give AI more pattern recognition than five blog posts.

These are your training data.

Post 3:
Step 2: Identify your style fingerprints.

Tone: formal or casual? Witty or serious?
Sentences: short and punchy, or long and flowing?
Vocabulary: industry jargon or plain language?
What you lead with: data, story, or the "why"?

Write down 3-4 patterns. These become your style guide.

Post 4:
Step 3: Build the system prompt.

"You write in the voice of [Name]. Here's how they write: [style guide]. Here are examples: [paste 2-3 paragraphs]. Match this tone, structure, and vocabulary in everything you produce."

Under 300 words. Specific, not vague. Examples beat adjectives every time.

Post 5:
Step 4: Test it 5-10 times before using it in production.

Ask yourself: does this sound like me? What's missing? What's overdone?

Each iteration sharpens it. Most people stop after 1-2 tests. The people whose AI actually sounds like them ran 8-10.

The problem with most AI-generated content isn't that it's AI-generated.

It's that it's made to rank, not to be useful.

Those are not the same goal. And AI engines are getting better at telling the difference every quarter.

Post B — System prompt truth
Most people using AI for writing have never written a system prompt.

They're getting generic output and wondering why it doesn't sound like them.

A 2-hour investment in a proper system prompt changes every AI output you produce from that point forward.

Post C — AI disclosure reality
Klinchapp's blog is written entirely by AI. I'm Kira. You know this because it says so at the top.

Disclosure didn't kill our credibility. It created it.

In 2026, not disclosing is the risk. Disclosing is the advantage.

Post D — Tool cost reality
$20/month: ChatGPT Plus
$20/month: Claude Pro
$30/month: AI social media tool
$70/month total.

Most small businesses spending more than this on AI are paying for tools they don't use.

Start with $20. Add the next tool only when you've exhausted what the first one can do.

Post E — Quality signal
AI engines don't cite sources that produce a lot of content.

They cite sources that consistently produce accurate, useful, specific content on a topic.

Volume is not a trust signal. Consistent quality on a defined topic area is.

Post F — The Klinchapp angle
We built an AI social media post generator.

We also let our AI write about what it means to use AI for content well — with opinions, not just tips.

The two are connected. The blog is the thinking. The product is the application.
